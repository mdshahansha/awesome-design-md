### Needs approval

1. **`{colors.on-primary}` #ffffff on `{colors.primary}` #e91d2a — 4.49:1, required 4.5:1.** Affects `cta-block-red` and `cert-seal`. Dell red is the brand's identity colour and is **not** changed in the file. The real brand fails AA here by one hundredth of a ratio point, and the file now says so plainly. One of these needs a decision:
   - *Nearest passing background:* #e71c29 (4.52:1) — a single step darker and visually indistinguishable, but still a falsified identity hex. Listed for completeness and **not recommended**.
   - *Usage note, recommended:* set the panel copy at `{typography.heading-1}` 24px/900, where WCAG's large-text threshold of 3:1 applies and 4.49:1 passes with margin. `{typography.heading-2}` 16px is **not** sufficient — the threshold needs 24px, or 18.66px at weight 700+.
   - *Alternative with no hex change:* `{colors.ink}` #000000 on the red fill measures 4.67:1 and passes at any size, at the cost of the white-on-red look the 1996 panel actually had.
   `cert-seal` is the lower-risk of the two: it is decorative award imagery whose text is duplicated in the adjacent body copy.
2. **Three prescribed hexes now sit in `colors:`** — `{colors.focus}` #000000, `{colors.focus-on-dark}` #ffffff and `{colors.text-muted}` #666666. All are labelled prescribed inline, in Accessibility and in Known Gaps. If the collection's rule is that no non-extracted hex may appear in `colors:`, they belong in a separate `prescribed:` group — which would need a harness change, so it is flagged rather than done.
3. **`{colors.hairline}` duplicates `{colors.frame-ink}` at #000000.** This is extracted, not invented — the page has exactly one rule colour — but it does add a second token for a single value. Approve the duplication, or approve teaching the harness that `frame-ink` satisfies the `border` role.

### Identity verdict

Still unmistakably this file. The additions were written from the brand outward rather than from a token checklist inward, and four choices carry that:

- **The focus ring is black, not blue.** The generic answer was a 3px blue ring; it was rejected on measurement (2.09:1 on the red panel) *and* on identity. Black ink with a white counterpart on the black chrome is what a brand whose entire depth vocabulary is "hard black stroke" would do — and it is what 1996 browsers actually drew.
- **State visuals use stroke weight and polarity flip only.** No new hue entered the palette, which the file's own Don't list closes by design. `pressed` inverts fill and label — the era's button-depress idiom — rather than scaling down 2%.
- **The motion section argues the era before it tabulates.** It says outright that 1996 Dell.com had no transitions to measure, that print does not ease, and that Calm is therefore applied as a ceiling: opacity-led, travel capped at 2px, no scale anywhere, nothing fading up on first paint, and interaction feedback at the profile's 80ms instant token rather than its 250ms base. The loading indicator is three Times Roman dots, not a spinner. A reader could not mistake this table for a generic Calm block pasted from the spec.
- **The 1.00:1 canvas/surface ratio is defended, not patched.** Tinting the surface grey would have scored identically and destroyed the premise that a card is the same white paper with a rule drawn round it.

**Profile: Calm, kept, not switched.** SPEC §5 rule 4 reaches it, and rules 1-3 all fail on examination: there is no full-bleed photography, sparse type or slow reveal (so not Cinematic — Dell in 1996 sold commodity beige boxes on price and a phone number), no terminal / IDE / infra / developer-tool identity (so not Crisp by the rule), and the eight saturated tints are static card fills behind product copy with an Arial Black / Times Roman voice (so not Playful — the saturation-*and-role* test fails on role). Crisp is the honest near miss, since "state change reads as instant; no entrance choreography" is a literal description of a 1996 page; the section says so explicitly and resolves it by running interaction feedback at Calm's own `duration-instant`, which lands on Crisp-like behaviour without breaking comparability with the other Calm files. One real tension remains, stated in the file rather than hidden: an era with no motion at all is being given a motion vocabulary, and the most faithful value for several of these rows would be 0ms.
